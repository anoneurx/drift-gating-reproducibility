% ABSTRACT
% =====================================================================
\noindent{\small\bfseries\itshape Abstract---}{\small\bfseries
Discrete policy switching is a recurring design pattern in adaptive cache and page replacement: monitor a workload for drift, rank candidate policies in shadow caches, and switch when a candidate outperforms the incumbent. We bound the achievable headroom of discrete switching using an exact Viterbi dynamic-programming (DP) hindsight oracle under an \emph{independent-replay model} across 88 traces (30 workload families), 21 baseline policies, and cache capacities from $1\%$ to $50\%$ of working-set size (WSS). At an assumed full-refill penalty ($c_{\text{switch}}=C$, $W=50{,}000$), \textbf{0 of 90} family--capacity groups ($1\%$--$10\%$ WSS) clear a $0.02$ net miss-ratio reduction (trace-weighted mean $0.0001$, family-macro mean $0.0001$, max $0.0067$); across $1\%$--$50\%$ WSS, only $2/440$ pairs (both at $25\%$ WSS) clear $0.02$. Under assumed lower migration penalties ($c_{\text{switch}} \le 0.10C$) and fine windows ($W=5{,}000$), $13/264$ pairs ($4.9\%$) clear $0.02$, and $0/264$ clear $0.02$ once $c_{\text{switch}} \ge 0.50C$ across $W \in \{5\text{k},10\text{k},25\text{k},50\text{k}\}$. Static S3-FIFO achieves the lowest global mean miss ratio ($0.4890$ at $59.5$\,ns/ref across $1\%$--$10\%$ WSS) and outperforms all 17 online switching controllers ($+0.0393$ to $+0.0433$ higher miss ratio; family-level $n=30$ two-sided Holm-adjusted $p \le 1.2\times 10^{-4}$, rank-biserial $r_b \ge 0.91$, 95\% family-cluster bootstrap CI excluding zero), as well as noise-free ($1.0C$), persistent-shadow ($+0.0175$), and 21-policy-pool ($+0.0470$) ablations, because cold-window shadow evaluation mis-ranks the top-1 policy in $32\%$ of evaluation windows. An exploratory leak-free leave-one-family-out ranker distinguishes rare zero-cost headroom ($13/88$ traces) with $\text{AUC}=0.895$ (PR-AUC $=0.471$) but $0.493$ balanced accuracy ($F_1=0.000$) due to class imbalance.\par}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
% =====================================================================

Page and cache replacement determines which blocks remain resident when memory is constrained. Because real workloads alternate among sequential scans, hot-object reuse, and random I/O, no single static eviction rule is optimal in every interval. This observation has motivated decades of adaptive replacement designs, from SEQ~\cite{seq} and EELRU~\cite{eelru} to ARC~\cite{arc}, LeCaR~\cite{lecar}, CACHEUS~\cite{cacheus}, and recent learning-augmented policies~\cite{lrb,l3cache,s4fifo}.

A recurring architecture in this literature is \emph{discrete policy switching}: monitor the miss stream for workload drift, evaluate a pool of candidate policies via sampled shadow caches, and switch the active policy when a candidate outperforms the incumbent. Each transition incurs metadata migration or cache warm-up overhead, while the hoped-for benefit is a lower miss ratio in the new regime. Whether that benefit exceeds transition costs---especially against modern scan-resistant static baselines such as S3-FIFO~\cite{s3fifo} and SIEVE~\cite{sieve}---is rarely bounded explicitly.

We ask one concrete question: \emph{when, if ever, does discrete policy switching beat the best static policy once switching costs are counted?} Rather than proposing a new switching controller, we compute a Viterbi dynamic-programming (DP) hindsight oracle under an \emph{independent-replay model} across 88 traces and compare it against both the per-pair best static policy and the global best static policy (S3-FIFO). We also evaluate 17 online switching controllers, diagnose why they underperform static S3-FIFO, and test whether workload features predict rare switching headroom.

\textbf{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Model-Relative Oracle Ceiling:} An exact Viterbi DP ceiling under independent replay across 88 traces ($1\%$--$50\%$ WSS), 9 normalized switch-cost fractions ($c_{\text{switch}} \in [0,C]$), and 4 window sizes ($W \in \{5\text{k},10\text{k},25\text{k},50\text{k}\}$), plus aggregation-sensitivity checks across trace-, family-, source-, and non-stationary-weighted averages.
\item \textbf{Simulator \& Oracle Validation:} Unit validation of core replacement policies against hand-worked traces, brute-force $P^T$ verification of the Viterbi DP, and a state-carrying vs.\ independent-replay comparison quantifying cold-transition penalties.
\item \textbf{Cluster-Aware Controller Evaluation \& Failure Diagnosis:} A matched comparison of 17 online controllers plus 4 shadow ablations and a 21-policy expansion (\code{ContShadow-21}) against static S3-FIFO using family-level ($n=30$) cluster inference, direct shadow-ranking accuracy across $9{,}564$ windows, and measured \code{switchTo()} migration latency.
\item \textbf{Exploratory Screening \& Reproducible Artifact:} A leak-free leave-one-family-out (LOFO) screening analysis separating ranking ($\text{AUC}=0.895$) from classification ($F_1=0.000$), together with a public artifact repository.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Background and Related Work}
\label{sec:related}
% =====================================================================

Adaptive cache replacement spans three paradigms: (i)~continuous internal adaptation, such as ARC~\cite{arc}, CAR~\cite{car}, and DIP set-dueling~\cite{dip}; (ii)~online expert weighting (Hedge~\cite{hedge}) over two policies, as in ACME~\cite{acme02}, LeCaR~\cite{lecar}, and CACHEUS~\cite{cacheus}; and (iii)~epoch-based discrete policy switching driven by drift detectors (CUSUM~\cite{page}, Page--Hinkley~\cite{page}, ADWIN~\cite{adwin}, Gama et al.~\cite{gama}) and sampled shadow caches (SHARDS~\cite{shards}, miniature simulations~\cite{mini}, Acme~\cite{acme}). Recent learned and learning-augmented policies---including Hawkeye~\cite{hawkeye}, LRB~\cite{lrb}, 3L-Cache~\cite{l3cache}, LPR~\cite{lpr}, EACLOCK~\cite{eaclock}, Clock2Q+~\cite{clock2q}, and S4FIFO~\cite{s4fifo}---target single-policy robustness, while S3-FIFO~\cite{s3fifo} and SIEVE~\cite{sieve} demonstrate that lightweight FIFO-based scan resistance rivals complex adaptive structures. Large-scale studies such as Twitter's cache analysis~\cite{twitter} motivate evaluating across diverse workload regimes, and emerging GPU/eBPF memory schedulers~\cite{msched,gpuext} revive interest in runtime policy selection. Unlike prior controller proposals, our work provides an empirical ceiling study bounded by an independent-replay DP oracle and a cluster-aware failure diagnosis of discrete switching.

\section{Problem Formulation}
\label{sec:problem}
% =====================================================================

Let a trace be a sequence of page references $r_1, \dots, r_N$ with working-set size $\text{WSS} = |\{r_1,\dots,r_N\}|$ and cache capacity $C = \max(1, \operatorname{round}(f \cdot \text{WSS}))$ for capacity fraction $f \in \{0.01, 0.05, 0.10, 0.25, 0.50\}$. After a warm-up prefix of $N_{\text{warm}} = \min(100{,}000, \lfloor 0.1 N \rfloor)$ references, the remaining $N_{\text{eval}} = N - N_{\text{warm}}$ references are partitioned into $T = \lfloor N_{\text{eval}} / W \rfloor$ complete windows of length $W$ (trailing incomplete references $N_{\text{eval}} \bmod W$ are omitted so every window has equal weight).

We distinguish four quantities across a policy pool $\mathcal{P}$ ($|\mathcal{P}|=21$):
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Best Global Static Policy:} The single policy $p_{\text{glob}}^* \in \mathcal{P}$ minimizing mean miss ratio across all $(i, f)$ pairs (here, \textbf{S3-FIFO}).
\item \textbf{Per-Pair Best Static Policy:} $p_{i,f}^* = \arg\min_{p \in \mathcal{P}} \text{MR}_{i,f,p}$, chosen ex post for each trace--capacity pair. By Jensen's inequality, $\mathbb{E}[\min_p \text{MR}_{i,f,p}] \le \min_p \mathbb{E}[\text{MR}_{i,f,p}]$.
\item \textbf{Independent-Replay Hindsight Oracle:} The minimum-cost schedule $(p_1,\dots,p_T) \in \mathcal{P}^T$ computed over independently replayed per-window miss counts $m_{t,p}$ with fixed transition penalty $c_{\text{switch}}$.
\item \textbf{State-Carrying Switching Execution:} Executing a schedule $(p_1,\dots,p_T)$ in a single live cache where switching at window boundaries transfers resident page IDs and coarse recency/frequency metadata via \code{switchTo()}.
\end{itemize}

Our primary confirmatory hypotheses (recorded locally in git before running the study, without external third-party preregistration and hence treated as post hoc) tested: \textbf{H1}, whether the oracle clears a $0.02$ net miss-ratio gain on $\ge 2/3$ of traces in any family--capacity group; \textbf{H2}, whether any online controller beats static S3-FIFO; and \textbf{H3} (exploratory), whether trace features predict zero-cost headroom on unseen families.

\section{Mathematical Model}
\label{sec:model}
% =====================================================================

Under the independent-replay model, each policy $p \in \mathcal{P}$ processes the full trace $[1, N_{\text{warm}} + TW]$ with its own warm cache state, producing per-window miss counts $m_{t,p}$ for $t = 1,\dots,T$. Given normalized switch penalty $c_{\text{switch}} = \beta C$ ($\beta \in [0, 1]$), the Viterbi dynamic program solves:
\begin{align}
\text{dp}[1][p] &= m_{1,p}, \label{eq:dp_init}\\
\text{dp}[t][p] &= m_{t,p} + \min_{q \in \mathcal{P}} \Bigl(\text{dp}[t-1][q] + c_{\text{switch}} \cdot \mathbf{1}[p \ne q]\Bigr), \label{eq:dp_rec}
\end{align}
for $t = 2,\dots,T$. Initial policy selection at $t=1$ is free. Ties in $\text{stay} \le \text{switch}$ break in favor of staying ($\mathbf{1}[p \ne q]=0$) to avoid zero-gain switches. Using running best and second-best predecessors $(q_1, q_2)$ per window, \eqref{eq:dp_rec} runs in $\mathcal{O}(TP)$ time and $\mathcal{O}(TP)$ space. Defining $S = \sum_{t=2}^T \mathbf{1}[p_t^* \ne p_{t-1}^*]$, the oracle objective ratio is $J^* = (\sum_{t=1}^T m_{t,p_t^*} + S \cdot c_{\text{switch}}) / (TW)$, and the net gain over the per-pair best static policy is $g(c_{\text{switch}}) = \text{MR}(p_{i,f}^*) - J^*$.

\textbf{Necessary break-even condition (Proposition 1).} A single switch paying $c_{\text{switch}}$ achieves positive net gain only if its cumulative miss reduction across the subsequent dwell $\Delta M \ge c_{\text{switch}}$, i.e., mean per-window miss-ratio reduction $\overline{\Delta m} > c_{\text{switch}} / (W \cdot L)$ over $L$ windows. For $c_{\text{switch}} = C = 5{,}000$ and $W = 50{,}000$, one switch requires $>10\%$ miss-ratio reduction in a single window just to break even.

\textbf{What the model does and does not establish.} Because $m_{t,p}$ comes from independent warm caches, the oracle ignores cold-cache and missing-ghost-state penalties upon switching; thus $g(c_{\text{switch}}=0)$ upper-bounds zero-penalty switching, while $g(c_{\text{switch}})$ is an upper bound for any real controller whose transition cost is at least $c_{\text{switch}}$ additional misses. It is \emph{not} a universal bound if a real switch avoids misses below independent replay (impossible for cold transitions) or incurs cascades exceeding $c_{\text{switch}}$.

\section{Experimental Methodology}
\label{sec:method}
% =====================================================================

All experiments run in a single-file C++17 simulator (\code{src/pathA_sim.cpp}, 1,900 lines) implementing 21 policies: \code{S3-FIFO}~\cite{s3fifo}, \code{SIEVE}~\cite{sieve}, \code{ARC}~\cite{arc}, \code{CAR}~\cite{car}, \code{LIRS}~\cite{lirs}, \code{W-TinyLFU}~\cite{wtinylfu}, \code{2Q}~\cite{twoq}, \code{CLOCK-Pro}~\cite{clockpro}, \code{LeCaR}~\cite{lecar}, \code{CACHEUS}~\cite{cacheus}, \code{LHD}~\cite{lhd}, \code{DIP-set-duel}~\cite{dip}, \code{LPR-style}~\cite{lpr}, \code{3L-style}~\cite{l3cache}, \code{MGLRU-style}~\cite{mglru}, \code{LRB-style} (online SGD~\cite{lrb}), \code{LRU}, \code{FIFO}, \code{CLOCK}, \code{LFU-A}, and \code{MRU} (compared against Belady-inspired oracle bounds~\cite{belady}).

We evaluate 17 online switching controllers over a 6-policy pool (\code{LRU}, \code{LFU-A}, \code{2Q}, \code{MRU}, \code{S3-FIFO}, \code{ARC}): 3 budgets (low/med/high) each for \code{CUSUM} ($h\in\{8,5,3\}$), \code{Page-Hinkley} ($\lambda\in\{9,5,2\}$), \code{ADWIN} ($\delta\in\{2\times 10^{-4},2\times 10^{-3},2\times 10^{-2}\}$), \code{Periodic} ($10,20,40$ epochs), and \code{Random} ($p_{\text{trig}}\in\{0.025,0.05,0.10\}$, seed \code{99}), plus \code{OracleTrig} (oracle switch boundaries) and \code{ContShadow} (every 10 epochs). Epoch length is $2{,}000$ references; shadow caches sample $20\%$ by page hash with $\Cs = \min(\lfloor 0.2C\rceil, \max(8,\lfloor 0.5\Dw\rceil))$, $40\%$ shadow warm-up, and $z=1.28$ lower-confidence-bound selection over 4 sub-blocks. In \code{switchTo()}, resident page IDs sorted by last access timestamp and access counts are loaded into the new policy; policy-specific ghost lists (\code{S3-FIFO} ghost, \code{ARC} $B_1/B_2$, \code{2Q} $A_{1\text{out}}$) cannot be preserved across heterogeneous policies. We also test 4 shadow ablations (\code{FullShadow}, \code{FullShadow-CostAware}, \code{PersistentShadow}, \code{PersistentShadow-CostAware} with $p=1.0, \Cs=C$) and \code{ContShadow-21} (all 21 policies).

\textbf{Switch-cost modeling.} We treat $c_{\text{switch}} = \beta C$ across $\beta \in \{0, 0.02, 0.05, 0.10, 0.15, 0.20, 0.25, 0.50, 1.00\}$ as \emph{assumed normalized cost scenarios} rather than empirically calibrated hardware penalties, and separately measure actual in-memory \code{switchTo()} migration latency in C++.

\section{Simulator and Oracle Validation}
\label{sec:validation}
% =====================================================================

We implemented an automated C++ and Python validation suite (\code{tests/validate_simulator.cpp}, \code{tests/run_tests.py}):
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Hand-worked policy sequences ($C=3$):} Exact hit/miss string matches for \code{LRU} (10 misses), \code{FIFO} (9 misses), and \code{MRU} (7 misses) on the 12-reference Belady sequence $\{1,2,3,4,1,2,5,1,2,3,4,5\}$, plus repeated-reference tests ($C=1,2$) for \code{LRU}, \code{CLOCK}, \code{S3-FIFO}, \code{ARC}, and \code{SIEVE}.
\item \textbf{Scan resistance \& monotonicity:} Verified \code{S3-FIFO} and \code{ARC} incur fewer misses than \code{LRU} on hot+scan sequences, and \code{S3-FIFO} miss counts decrease monotonically across $C \in \{5, 10, 20\}$.
\item \textbf{Viterbi DP vs.\ brute force:} Exhaustive $P^T$ enumeration ($P=4, T=5$, $1{,}024$ schedules) matched \code{solveDPOracle()} to $10^{-12}$ across $c_{\text{switch}} \in \{0, 15, 35, 100\}$.
\item \textbf{State-carrying vs.\ independent replay:} On a 3-phase trace ($3\times 2{,}000$ refs, $C=10$: hot $\{0..9\}$ $\to$ cyclic scan $\{100..115\}$ $\to$ hot $\{0..9\}$), independent-replay $c=0$ oracle scored $52$ misses ($0.0087$) whereas state-carrying \code{switchTo()} execution incurred $2{,}011$ misses ($0.3352$), confirming that real transitions incur substantial cold/state-loss overhead above the independent-replay bound.
\item \textbf{Bounds guard:} Fixed a bug in \code{src/pathA_sim.cpp} where passing \code{--np} smaller than $\max(r_i)+1$ caused out-of-bounds access in unit tests. External cross-validation against \code{libCacheSim} was not performed (listed as an unresolved item).
\end{itemize}
All 16 C++ validation checks and all 5 Python CLI unit tests (\code{make test}) pass.

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}
% =====================================================================

\textbf{H1: Oracle Switching Ceiling.} Across 264 primary pairs ($1\%$--$10\%$ WSS, $W=50\text{k}$), \textbf{0 of 90} family--capacity groups clear $g(C) \ge 0.02$ on $\ge 2/3$ of traces (Table~\ref{tab:h1}). Importantly, this null result is \emph{not} an artifact of the $68\%$ kernel share: across the 30 independent families, family-macro mean $g(C)=0.0001$ ($g(0)=0.0018$); across 4 equal-weighted sources, $g(C)=0.0001$ ($g(0)=0.0024$); and even restricting strictly to the $21$ non-stationary traces with $g(0)>0.001$, mean $g(C)=0.0002$ (max $0.0067$) and mean $g(0)=0.0049$ (max $0.0564$).

\section{Discussion, Threats to Validity, and Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}
% =====================================================================

\textbf{Design implication.} Discrete policy switching rarely clears a $0.02$ net gain even under an optimistic independent-replay oracle, and all evaluated online controllers lose to static \code{S3-FIFO} because cold-window shadow evaluation mis-ranks policies in $\sim 32\%$ of windows while heterogeneous \code{switchTo()} discards ghost state. Systems builders gain more from adopting a strong scan-resistant static policy (\code{S3-FIFO} or \code{CAR}) than from adding drift-triggered switching machinery.

\textbf{Threats to validity \& limitations.} (1)~\emph{Independent-replay model:} Does not model state-carrying cold cascades; our validation trace confirms state-carrying execution incurs higher misses than $c=0$ independent replay. (2)~\emph{Trace corpus:} 68\% instrumented kernels average 0.30M refs, FIU traces have $\ge 31\%$ compulsory misses, and \code{LD_PRELOAD} omits post-map kernel page faults. (3)~\emph{External cross-validation:} \code{libCacheSim} bit-for-bit cross-validation was not run. (4)~\emph{Scope:} Windows $<5\text{k}$ and live kernel implementations (MGLRU / \code{cache_ext}) are outside scope. (5)~\emph{Post-hoc status:} Local git timestamping is not external preregistration, and H3's $0.005$ threshold was post hoc.

% =====================================================================
\section{Reproducibility and Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
% =====================================================================

\textbf{Reproducibility and data availability.} All simulator code (\code{src/pathA_sim.cpp}), validation tests (\code{make test}, \code{make verify}), raw CSVs, supplement, and table/figure scripts are publicly available at \url{https://github.com/anoneurx/drift-gating-reproducibility}.

\textbf{Conclusion.} Under an independent-replay Viterbi DP oracle across 88 traces ($1\%$--$50\%$ WSS), discrete policy switching clears a $0.02$ net gain in $0/90$ family--capacity groups ($1\%$--$10\%$ WSS) at $c_{\text{switch}}=C$, $2/440$ pairs across $1\%$--$50\%$ WSS at $c_{\text{switch}}=C$, $4.9\%$ at $0.10C$ ($W=5\text{k}$), and $0/264$ once $c_{\text{switch}} \ge 0.50C$. Static \code{S3-FIFO} dominates all 17 switching controllers ($p_{\text{fam,Holm}} \le 1.2\times 10^{-4}$), persistent $1.0C$ ablations, and \code{ContShadow-21} because shadow ranking fails on $32\%$ of windows and heterogeneous switches discard ghost metadata.
